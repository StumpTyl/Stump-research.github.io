## Research Agenda

My research integrates **engineering education**, **psychometrics**, and **computing education research**, with a central focus on advancing **inclusive and justice‑oriented approaches to assessment and validation**. I am particularly interested in how quantitative methods can be designed and interpreted to better represent diverse student experiences and learning outcomes in engineering and computing contexts.

My dissertation, *“Informing Our Prior Beliefs”: Actualizing Bayesian Item Response Theory as a Critical Quantitative Methodology of Validation for the MATLAB Concept Inventory (MCS1)*, advances **Bayesian Item Response Theory (IRT)** as a critical quantitative methodology. This work emphasizes posterior predictive checking, differential item functioning, and intersectional validation as tools for improving the epistemic and ethical quality of assessment in engineering education.

---

## Research Areas

**Engineering and Computing Education Research**  
I study student learning and affect in engineering and computing courses, with particular attention to first‑year experiences, multidisciplinary design, and computational learning environments.

**Inclusive Curriculum and Assessment Design**  
My work examines how assessment design decisions shape whose knowledge is recognized and validated, emphasizing equity‑focused and justice‑oriented approaches to instrument development.

**Psychometric Validation and Measurement**  
I conduct methodological research on affective and conceptual instrument development, using confirmatory factor analysis, structural equation modeling, and Bayesian and classical IRT frameworks.

**Assessment in Entrepreneurial‑Minded Learning**  
I investigate the measurement of constructs such as connections, curiosity, and creating value, focusing on validity evidence across institutional and curricular contexts.
