I am a PhD Candidate in **Engineering Education** at **The Ohio State University**, specializing in **quantitative research methods**. My research focuses on the development and validation of inclusive assessments in engineering and computing education, with particular emphasis on **psychometric validation**, **Bayesian item response theory**, and **justice‑oriented approaches to measurement**.

My dissertation, *“Informing Our Prior Beliefs”: Actualizing Bayesian Item Response Theory as a Critical Quantitative Methodology of Validation for the MATLAB Concept Inventory (MCS1)*, advances Bayesian IRT as a critical quantitative methodology for supporting intersectional validation, posterior predictive checking, and differential item functioning analysis in concept inventories.

Broadly, my research agenda spans:
- Engineering and computing education research  
- Inclusive curriculum and assessment design  
- Affective and conceptual instrument development  
- Structural equation modeling and multilevel modeling  
- Assessment in entrepreneurial‑minded learning  

Methodologically, I draw on both **quantitative** and **qualitative** traditions, including structural equation modeling, confirmatory factor analysis, multilevel modeling, Bayesian psychometrics, thematic analysis, and grounded theory. I aim to make advanced quantitative methods accessible and meaningful for interdisciplinary education research audiences.

At The Ohio State University, I serve as a **Graduate Research Assistant** in the Department of Engineering Education and a **Graduate Teaching Associate** in the Multidisciplinary Engineering Capstone Design sequence. I have previously conducted research in biosystems engineering, microbial risk assessment, and physics education research.

I am especially interested in collaborative work that bridges methodology, equity, and practice in engineering education.
